## Introducción
En B2 se espera **participar en un debate estructurado**: presentar una postura con argumentos, responder a los del otro, **matizar** el acuerdo o desacuerdo y llegar a conclusiones.

## Explicación central
### Presentar una postura
*I'd argue that… / It seems to me that… / I'm convinced that… / From my perspective…*

### Matizar
- *To some extent… / In some cases… / It depends on… / That's true up to a point.*
- *I'm in favour of X, provided that… / as long as…*

### Responder al otro
- Concesión: *I take your point, but… / Granted, … however…*
- Contraargumento: *On the other hand… / The problem with that is… / That may be true, but…*
- Pedir aclaración: *What exactly do you mean by…? / Could you give an example?*

### Interrumpir y retomar
- *Sorry to interrupt, but… / Can I just add something? / Going back to what I said…*

### Llegar a un acuerdo
*So, shall we agree that…? / It looks like we both think… / Let's say…*

### Hablar de ventajas y desventajas
*The main advantage/drawback is… / On the plus side… / The downside is…*

## Errores comunes de hispanohablantes
1. Repetir siempre *I think*: variar con *I'd argue, It seems to me*.
2. ❌ *Depends of* ✅ *It depends on*.
3. Interrumpir sin fórmula: suena brusco.
4. ❌ *I'm agree with you in part.* ✅ *I partly agree with you.*

## Comparación con el español
«Depende de» = *It depends on* (no *of*). «Hasta cierto punto» = *up to a point / to some extent*. «Siempre y cuando» = *provided that / as long as*.

## Ejemplo extendido en contexto
*A: Should cars be banned from city centres?*
*B: **I'd argue that** it's a good idea **as long as** public transport improves.*
*A: **I take your point, but** what about people with disabilities?*
*B: **That's true up to a point** — there could be exceptions. **So, shall we agree that** a partial ban is the best option?*
