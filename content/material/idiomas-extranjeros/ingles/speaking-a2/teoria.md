## Introducción
En A2 se espera poder **describir rutinas, personas, lugares y experiencias simples** con frases cortas y conectores básicos, y mantener una conversación sencilla sobre temas cotidianos.

## Explicación central
### Describir rutinas (present simple + frecuencia)
*I usually get up at 7. I always have breakfast at home. On Saturdays I play football.*
- Secuencia: *first, then, after that, finally*.

### Describir personas
- Apariencia: *She's tall. He has short dark hair and blue eyes. He wears glasses.*
- Personalidad: *She's friendly / kind / funny / shy / hard-working.*
- *What's she like?* (cómo es) ≠ *What does she look like?* (cómo es físicamente) ≠ *What does she like?* (qué le gusta).

### Describir lugares
*There is / there are…, It's near…, It's a small / quiet / busy town. My favourite place is… because…*

### Mantener la conversación
- Pedir que repitan: *Sorry, can you repeat that? / Could you speak more slowly?*
- Ganar tiempo: *Well…, Let me think…*
- Devolver la pregunta: *And you? / What about you?*

### Conectores básicos
*and, but, because, so, also*.

## Errores comunes de hispanohablantes
1. Olvidar la *-s*: ❌ *She live in Rosario.* ✅ *She lives…*
2. ❌ *She has 15 years.* ✅ *She is 15 (years old).*
3. Confundir *What is she like?* con *What does she like?*
4. ❌ *I am agree.* ✅ *I agree.*

## Comparación con el español
«Tener… años» se dice con *be*: *I'm 16*. «¿Cómo es?» tiene dos traducciones según se pregunte por la personalidad (*What's she like?*) o por el aspecto (*What does she look like?*).

## Ejemplo extendido en contexto
*My best friend is Tomás. He's 16 and he's very funny. He's tall and he has curly hair. We usually meet after school and play video games. On Sundays we go to the park because it's near my house. What about you?*
